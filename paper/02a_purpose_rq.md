# Purpose and Research Questions

<!-- Allocation: ~500 words (included in Lit Review allocation) -->

This study examines the association between in-person socialization frequency and volunteering across generational cohorts using four waves of nationally representative data from the Current Population Survey Civic Engagement and Volunteering Supplement (2017, 2019, 2021, 2023). We employ an integrated <red>analytical</red> design that combines variable-centered regression and person-centered profiling to <red>achieve</red> methodological triangulation <red>and strengthen inference</red>. By spanning four waves that bracket the COVID-19 pandemic, <red>the design enables us to</red> distinguish structural cohort effects from pandemic-specific disruptions. <red>Guided by this framework, the study addresses two complementary research questions: differential associations across generations and the underlying civic engagement profiles that characterize participation patterns.</red>

<blue>The dependent variable is volunteering status (volunteered in the past 12 months). The independent variable is in-person socialization frequency. Generational cohort serves as the primary moderator of the socialization-volunteering association. Education, employment status, civic social media use, and the COVID-19 period are examined as exploratory moderators through three-way interactions (see Figure 1).</blue>

**Research Question 1** (Variable-Centered): How does the association between in-person socialization frequency and volunteering differ across generational cohorts, and do education, employment, civic social media use, and the COVID-19 pandemic moderate this association differently by generation?

- *H1.* Higher levels of in-person socialization will be positively associated with volunteering across all generational cohorts.
- *H2.* Generation Z will exhibit a weaker <red>association between socialization and volunteering at higher levels of socialization than</red> older cohorts (i.e., a plateau effect).
- *H3a.* Education will positively moderate the <red>association between socialization and volunteering,</red> but this moderating effect will be weaker for Generation Z than for older cohorts.
- *H3b.* Employment will positively moderate the <red>association between socialization and volunteering,</red> with effects that are relatively consistent across generations.
- *H3c.* Civic social media use will positively moderate the <red>association between socialization and volunteering,</red> but the moderating effect will be stronger for older cohorts than for Generation Z.
- *H3d.* The <red>association between socialization and volunteering</red> will be stable across the pre- and post-COVID periods, <red>supporting a cohort-based rather than period-based interpretation.</red>

**Research Question 2** (Person-Centered): What distinct civic engagement typologies emerge from patterns of boycotting, contacting officials, <red>in-person socialization, organizational membership, and charitable donation</red>, and how are generational cohorts distributed across these typologies?

- *H4.* Generation Z will be disproportionately concentrated in low-engagement profiles characterized by minimal socialization and low organizational membership.
- *H5.* High-engagement profiles will show higher volunteering rates and be overrepresented among older cohorts.
