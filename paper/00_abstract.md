# Bowling Alone, Scrolling Together: In-Person Socialization, Social Isolation, and Volunteering Across Five Generational Cohorts

# Abstract

Social isolation has been declared a public health epidemic, yet its civic consequences remain underexamined. Using four waves of the Current Population Survey Civic Engagement and Volunteering Supplement (2017–2023; *N* = 201,168), this study combines survey-weighted logistic regression and latent profile analysis to examine whether in-person socialization predicts volunteering differently across five generational cohorts. Across all generations, the transition from no socialization to even occasional contact is associated with the largest marginal gain in <red>the probability of</red> volunteering (7–10 percentage points), a "First Step Effect" comparable in magnitude to the education gradient. Gen Z exhibits a paradox: the highest baseline volunteering probability at zero socialization yet a plateau beyond moderate socialization levels, a <red>ceiling effect</red> absent in older cohorts. These patterns predate COVID-19 and persist unchanged, establishing them as cohort characteristics. Education amplifies the socialization–volunteering gradient, but neither employment nor civic social media use alters the generational pattern. Over half of Gen Z occupies civic engagement profiles defined by social isolation. Findings suggest that declining youth volunteering reflects social disconnection rather than weakened civic motivation, with implications for low-threshold volunteer recruitment.

**Keywords:** volunteering, social isolation, civic engagement, generational differences, COVID-19, latent profile analysis, Current Population Survey
