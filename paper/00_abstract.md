# Bowling Alone, Scrolling Together: In-Person Socialization, Social Isolation, and Volunteering Across Five Generational Cohorts

# Abstract

Social isolation has been declared a public health epidemic, yet its civic consequences remain underexamined. Using four waves of the Current Population Survey Civic Engagement and Volunteering Supplement (2017–2023; *N* = 201,168) and three complementary analytic methods, this study examines whether in-person socialization predicts volunteering differently across generational cohorts. Three findings emerge. First, a universal threshold: across all five generations, the transition from no socialization to even occasional contact produces the largest marginal gain in volunteering probability (8–10 percentage points), a pattern we term the "First Step Effect." Second, Generation Z exhibits a unique ceiling: beyond moderate socialization, volunteering probability plateaus—a pattern absent in all older cohorts. Third, these patterns are temporally stable, present before the COVID-19 pandemic and unchanged afterward, establishing them as cohort characteristics rather than pandemic artifacts. Education amplifies the socialization–volunteering gradient (*p* = .009), but neither employment nor civic social media use alters the generational pattern. Over half of Gen Z occupies civic engagement profiles defined by social isolation, and socialization ranks as the single most important predictor of volunteering for Gen Z—not for any older generation. These findings are consistent with a structural account of declining youth volunteering, rooted in social disconnection rather than weakened civic motivation, with implications for low-threshold volunteer recruitment and community-based programming.

**Keywords:** volunteering, social isolation, civic engagement, generational differences, COVID-19, latent profile analysis, machine learning, Current Population Survey
